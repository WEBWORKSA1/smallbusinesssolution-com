A local marketing plan on a small budget

You don't need a big agency retainer to fill your calendar. You need a few channels done consistently, in the right order.

Step 1: Own your Google Business Profile

For local businesses, the free Google Business Profile is often the single highest-return marketing asset. Complete every field, choose accurate categories, add real photos, list services with prices or ranges, post updates, and keep hours current. Respond to every review — positive and negative — within a couple of days.

Step 2: Build a review engine

Reviews drive both rankings and trust. Ask at the moment of delight (job completed, product delivered, compliment received), send a direct link, and make it a routine — not an occasional campaign. Never buy or fake reviews; platforms remove them and it can damage your listing.

Step 3: Make referrals deliberate

Happy customers refer more when you make it easy. Give them a specific ask ("Do you know another restaurant owner who struggles with scheduling?") and a simple thank-you, such as a discount or a donation to a local cause.

Step 4: Basic local SEO on your website

  • A page for each core service, with the town or region you serve in the heading and text.
  • Name, address and phone number consistent everywhere online.
  • Fast loading on mobile, with click-to-call and a short contact form.
  • Real photos of your work and team.

Step 5: Start an email list on day one

Email is the channel you own. Collect addresses at checkout, on your website and at events with a clear benefit — a discount, a checklist, early access. Send one useful email a month at minimum.

Step 6: Partner with complementary businesses

A wedding photographer and a florist, a gym and a physiotherapist, a bookkeeper and a business lawyer: partner businesses serve the same customers without competing. Swap referrals, co-host events or bundle offers.

Step 7: Add paid ads only once the basics work

Paid search and social ads amplify what already converts. Start small with a tight radius and a single offer, track calls and form fills, and stop anything that can't show a cost per customer you're happy with.
